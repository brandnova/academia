If you're reading this, an admin has assigned you as a Moderator, School Representative, or both, for a hub on Academia. This page is your reference for what that means, day to day.

## The two roles, and how they differ

**Moderators** help keep questions answered. Your main job is watching the unanswered-questions queue for your hub and stepping in, or making sure someone does, when questions are sitting too long without a response.

**School Representatives** maintain the hub itself: managing departments, and deciding who else gets to be a moderator for that hub. A Representative is a coordinator for their school's presence on the platform, not a platform administrator.

You can hold both roles for the same hub at once, many people do.

## How you got this role

There's no self-service way to become a Moderator or Representative, an admin assigned you directly. If you're a Representative, you *can* self-assign as a Moderator for your own hub through the Moderators tab on your `/moderation` page, useful if your hub doesn't have a dedicated moderator yet.

## What Moderators can do

- View the unanswered-questions queue, scoped to your assigned hub(s)
- Answer questions yourself, or encourage others to
- Report problematic content for admin review
- Escalate a report that's already been filed, when it needs faster attention than the normal review queue
- Lock a question, in genuinely rare cases (see "Locking questions" below)

## What Moderators can't do

- Delete content directly
- Manage user accounts or suspend anyone
- Resolve reports yourself, reports go to admins
- Assign or remove other moderators (that's a Representative's job)

## What Representatives can additionally do

- Add or edit departments for your school
- Assign and remove moderators for your hub
- Everything a Moderator can do, for your own hub

## What Representatives can't do

- Assign other Representatives (admin-only)
- Resolve reports
- Edit school-level information (name, location, verification) directly, that's an admin action
- Suspend users

## Your day-to-day

**Check the unanswered queue regularly.** A question sitting unanswered for days is the exact problem Academia exists to solve, don't let it happen on your watch.

**Answer, don't just monitor.** You're not just a gatekeeper, you're expected to actually help answer questions when you're able to.

**Report, don't personally punish.** If you see spam, abuse, or misinformation, report it the same way any user would. Reports go to admins, who have the tools (and the full picture across the platform) to act on them properly.

**Recruit good contributors (Representatives).** If someone in your school is consistently giving helpful, accurate answers, consider whether they'd make a good moderator. You can find and assign them directly from your Moderators tab.

## Locking questions

Locking prevents new answers from being added. This is a genuinely rare tool, meant for a question that's become a spam magnet or is a fully resolved administrative matter with no further value in staying open. Marking a question "Solved" does **not** lock it, questions stay open to new answers and a reassigned best answer indefinitely by design, that's intentional, not a bug. Don't use locking as a way to end a disagreement in the comments, that's a reporting situation instead.

## Escalating a report

Escalating is different from reporting. Reporting flags content for admin review, any user can do that. Escalating marks a report that's already been filed as needing priority attention, and it's a Moderator/Representative action.

You can't escalate content directly, it has to already be reported first. If you spot something that needs admin attention and it hasn't been reported yet, report it the normal way, then escalate that report if it's urgent enough to jump the queue.

Use escalation for things like a clear abuse case, a spam campaign, or anything with real urgency, not as a way to speed up an otherwise routine report. A report that's already been resolved or rejected, or one that's already escalated, can't be escalated again.

## Marking best answers

You don't do this, ever, for a question that isn't yours. Only the question's own author can mark a best answer. If you think an answer deserves recognition, upvote it, that's the tool available to you.

## If your role is misused

Admins can remove your assignment at any time if the responsibilities outlined here aren't being met. This isn't punitive by default, sometimes people take on a role and find they don't have the time, that's fine, just let an admin know.
